### Problem and opportunity

The clinic currently books by phone, manages walk-ins at the desk, and loses doctor capacity to missed appointments. The MVP aims to make booking faster, give front-desk staff a live view of arrivals and appointments, and send consent-based reminders.

### Objectives and measures

| Objective | Target | Measurement |
|---|---:|---|
| Reduce booking effort | Online booking ≤2:30 vs. 6:18 illustrative phone baseline | Timed test bookings |
| Reduce missed appointments | Pilot no-show rate ≤12% vs. 18.0% illustrative baseline | Completed/missed appointments during pilot |
| Deliver a workable MVP | ≥95% UAT pass rate | UAT checklist |
| Make the workflow easy to use | SUS ≥75/100 | Anonymous UAT questionnaire |

### Scope

**In scope:** availability slots, patient contact/consent capture, online booking, conflict prevention, reminder workflow, front-desk dashboard, doctor schedule controls, no-show report, training and pilot support.

**Out of scope:** payments, clinical records, billing, diagnosis, prescriptions, EHR integration, live deployment with real patient data, and multi-clinic scaling.

### Business case

An appointment reminder and self-service booking prototype is low-cost compared with the potential value of reclaimed clinical time. If a five-doctor clinic schedules 300 appointments a week, a reduction from 18% to 12% no-shows represents about 18 additional attended appointments weekly. This is a planning hypothesis, not a forecast or a client claim. The decision is to fund a ten-week MVP/pilot, provided privacy controls and UAT readiness are achieved.
